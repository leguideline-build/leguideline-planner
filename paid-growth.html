How the forecast works

Simple on purpose, so you can check it.
  1. Each format’s cost per visit is what it actually cost across — and — combined. Spending above the most a format has ever had raises its cost, since bigger audiences are less warm.
  2. Followers = profile visits × the share that followed in —. Kept = followers minus unfollows, which have stayed flat whatever we spend.
  3. YouTube uses September’s costs for the subscriber campaign and October’s for views campaigns.
  4. Boosted posts aren’t included; plan them on top. A forecast from a few months of data is a direction, not a promise: check it against each new report.

      Break-even calculator

      Two assumptions, everything else from the reports.
      Lifetime revenue from a paying user. Read as $ for Instagram and € for YouTube.
      Share of followers kept who eventually pay. Creator products commonly see low single digits.

      YouTube shows new subscribers with no unsubscribes, so it looks cheaper than it is. Instagram’s October total adds the €64.94 boosted posts as dollars, without converting.

      To turn this into real ROI

      Four changes so next month’s report can show return, not just cost.
      1. 01Tag every link. UTM parameters on the bio link, Beta ad and YouTube descriptions, so site visits are tied to the channel and the ad.
      2. 02Capture something on the site. A waitlist or email sign-up on the landing page. That gives a cost per sign-up, the first number that sits close to revenue.
      3. 03Report kept followers, not gained. Keep the net figure as the headline, and log unfollows by week to see which posts cause them.
      4. 04Convert currency once. Report both channels in one currency at the month’s rate, so totals add up.
